How it works

Say you are starting

Open an issue with your language first, even before you translate a word. Then nobody does the same work twice, and we can tell you early if your alphabet needs work on the device (see below).

Translate the spreadsheet

Every string comes with its key and the English text, and the device ones with a length guide: the longest version used today, in characters. Fill the your_translation column, and write any question in note. Look at the English screens while you do it: a word means more next to the screen it sits on.

Check it on the screens

We build your language, render every screen and send you the sheet, so you see your text where it lands before anything is released. Too long somewhere? We come back to you with the place it goes. You are credited in the release notes if you want to be.

What your alphabet means for the device

The device screen is small and its letters are drawn from built-in letter sets. That decides how fast a language can ship.

Latin alphabet

Polish, Italian, Dutch, Portuguese, Turkish, Czech... The fastest path: the device shows plain letters only, so accents are dropped, as French, Spanish and Vietnamese do today (write a for ą, s for ş). Once the translation is in, it can go out with the next release.

Another alphabet

Cyrillic, Greek... The device needs a letter set for it first, narrow enough for the text to fit. That is real work on our side, so it starts once a translation is in hand, and takes longer.

Right-to-left or joined letters

Arabic, Hebrew, Thai, Hindi... These need more than a letter set. Ask in an issue before you translate, so we can tell you honestly what is possible.

PackScope has none of these limits for alphabets your PC already displays. The full guides, with the rules each tool follows, are on GitHub: PocketOBI and PackScope.
